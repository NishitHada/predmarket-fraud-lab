A prediction market is a bet on an event

You buy YES or NO shares priced 1–99¢. The price is the crowd's implied probability — 70¢ means "70% likely." Whoever's right is paid $1 per share at resolution; the other side gets $0. Buying NO is the same as betting against YES (a short).

The catch: three separate things can be rigged — independently.
📈
The price — faked with wash trades, pumps, spoofing and sock-puppets. A price is a claim, not proof.
⚖️
The settlement — whoever decides the outcome (the "oracle") can simply rule against you, no matter what the price said.
🏦
Your access — the platform can raise fees, freeze withdrawals, or trade against you.

Trust a market only as much as you trust its weakest layer: its liquidity, its traders, its oracle, and its operator.
